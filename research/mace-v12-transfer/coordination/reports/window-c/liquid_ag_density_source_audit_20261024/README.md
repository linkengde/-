# Liquid Ag density source search — 2026-10-24

## Candidate references found

**Abdullaev et al. (2020):** R. N. Abdullaev, R. A. Khairulin, and S. V. Stankus, “Density and thermal expansion of silver in the solid and liquid states,” *Journal of Physics: Conference Series* 1677 (2020), 012161. DOI: [10.1088/1742-6596/1677/1/012161](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/1677/1/012161). Crossref and OpenAlex metadata agree on the title, authors, journal, year, volume, and article number. The full IOP PDF was retrieved and inspected in this session; its first page states the Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 license. The paper reports pure-silver density and thermal expansion from 293 to 1631 K, including the phase transition, and estimates errors for its tabulated volumetric properties. This is a direct primary experimental source suitable for anchoring liquid-Ag candidate geometries within its stated liquid temperature range.

**Korobeinikov et al. (2018):** I. I. Korobeinikov, D. Chebykin, X. Yu, S. Seetharaman, and O. Volkova, “Density of tin, silver and copper,” *Archives of Materials Science and Engineering* 92(1) (2018), 28–32. DOI: [10.5604/01.3001.0012.5509](https://doi.org/10.5604/01.3001.0012.5509). Crossref and OpenAlex identify the same article. The abstract describes Archimedean measurements of liquid-metal densities from 700 to 1520°C, a range that includes 1250 K (976.85°C). It reports deviations of 0.5–1% from literature values depending on the metal; that is an agreement statement, not a pointwise measurement uncertainty. OpenAlex marks the article closed-access, so the silver table and uncertainty still need full-text inspection.

**Assael et al. (2012):** “Reference data for the density and viscosity of liquid antimony, bismuth, lead, nickel and silver,” *High Temperatures–High Pressures*. OpenAlex record [W2608312387](https://openalex.org/W2608312387) lists M. J. Assael, Jiangtao Wu, Erhard Kaschnitz, Konstantinos D. Antoniadis, W. A. Wakeham, Agni E. Kalyva, R. Michael Banish, and I. Egry, and points to the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki institutional repository record at `https://ikee.lib.auth.gr/record/217743`. OpenAlex supplies no DOI or PDF and marks the record closed-access. An earlier exact-title Crossref search did not resolve a DOI; a follow-up request on 2026-10-24 returned HTTP 429, so DOI resolution remains open.

**Nakajima (1973), open full text but not an independent pure-Ag reference:** Hiroshi Nakajima, “Densities of Liquid Silver-Cadmium and Silver-Indium Alloys,” *Journal of the Japan Institute of Metals* 37(4) (1973), 386–390. DOI: [10.2320/jinstmet1952.37.4_386](https://doi.org/10.2320/jinstmet1952.37.4_386); OpenAlex [W407048317](https://openalex.org/W407048317) marks the published version open access. The J-STAGE PDF was retrieved (HTTP 200; 377,978 bytes; SHA256 `01f44e40e5e82286ce728dd34144478c1233d019a5e6431515f8d29b7f7048f8`). The paper measured Ag-Cd/Ag-In alloys and fits specific volume as `v = v0(1 + αT)`; reported fit-parameter errors are ±0.1% for `v0` and ±0.5% for `α`, not a pointwise pure-Ag uncertainty. Crucially, page 387 says the pure-silver specific volume is taken from Kirshenbaum, Cahill, and Grosse (1962); Table 1 has no pure-Ag row. The plotted Ag curve is therefore a reproduction of that 1962 source, not an independent pure-Ag measurement. This article does not resolve `rho_Ag(1250 K)`.

### Abdullaev et al. full-text audit (2026-10-24)

- The IOP PDF was retrieved from `https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/1677/1/012161/pdf` (665,958 bytes; SHA256 `cd37e2b91be0369ebe1b9d72ffb7377a28dfc74171ed0dd3cd22892f95e23c3a`). It was inspected locally; the paper identifies the CC BY 3.0 license on page 1.
- The paper describes gamma-ray attenuation measurements on 99.99 mass% silver. It derives density using measured volume expansion in solid and liquid states, the relative density change at melting, and a room-temperature reference density from lattice parameters. Temperature calibration error is stated to be no more than 3 K.
- Equation (2) is `rho_m(T) = 9327.1 - 0.9956 * (T - 1234.93) kg m^-3`. The paper says its liquid-silver reference data cover 1234.93–1631 K and recommends them over that interval.
- At 1250 K, equation (2) gives `9312.096308 kg m^-3 = 9.312096308 g cm^-3`. This is a value calculated from the paper's fitted equation, not a separately listed measurement at exactly 1250 K. At 1600 K the same equation gives `8963.636308 kg m^-3 = 8.963636308 g cm^-3` (Table 1 rounds this to 8963.6 kg m^-3).
- Table 1 reports the liquid-density error as 0.25% at the melting point, 0.26% at 1300 K, and 0.34% at 1600 K. The paper does not specify a confidence/coverage convention for these percentages. For 1250 K, 0.25–0.26% is only a neighboring-row context (`approximately` 0.0233–0.0242 g cm^-3 if applied to the equation value), not an exact 1250 K uncertainty or 95% interval. Temperature calibration error is stated as no more than 3 K; it is not added again because the paper does not clarify whether it is already represented in the tabulated density-error estimates.
- The former EAM-derived 1250 K density, 8.867447227420516 g cm^-3, remains recorded as a legacy model output, not as the experimental reference. A [separate package](../abdullaev2020_liquid_ag_density_rescaled_20261024/README.md) contains 12 isotropically rescaled geometry-only copies at 1250 and 1600 K, targeting 0.97/1.00/1.03 times the temperature-specific equation value. Original candidates remain untouched. The 1050 K liquid snapshots are excluded because they lie below the 1234.93 K melting point and outside the paper's liquid-fit range; no liquid density was extrapolated there. None of these copies has a DFT label or has been added to a dataset.

## Current audit status

**Status: the liquid-density source gate is satisfied for geometry proposals at 1250 K and 1600 K.** Abdullaev et al. (2020) is a primary experimental source and its full CC BY 3.0 paper was inspected. Equation (2), its 1234.93–1631 K liquid range, the fitted 1250/1600 K densities, and the adjacent/tabulated error entries are now recorded above. The 0.9% Assael correlation statistic is not treated as a 1250 K point uncertainty. The 1973 Nakajima paper remains a secondary reproduction of the 1962 pure-Ag curve and does not count as an independent pure-silver measurement.

The legacy EAM-derived anchor remains unchanged in the historical candidate package. New copies use the experimental fit as a temperature-specific geometry reference at 1250 and 1600 K; no equation was extrapolated below melting for 1050 K. All copies remain geometry-only, without DFT labels or dataset integration. The outstanding B1 gates still include solid-Ag source/role review, the Ti₃SiC₂ source/role decision, a frozen DFT method and energy target, and V20 control completion by its owner. No DFT pilot is launched by this source audit.

The saved cloud-environment draft includes the IOP, Old City Publishing, OpenArchives, IKEE, and `www.jstage.jst.go.jp` hosts. It is still a draft and takes effect only after the user reviews/saves and publishes the environment configuration.
