# Ag/W XTANT-3 table inventory and source comparison

Date: 2026-11-24  
Purpose: organize the user-supplied Ag/W electronic-property tables for the exploratory melt/cool/resolidification TTM study. This is a provenance and table-consistency audit; it does not select a final parameterization or run TTM.

## Archived source files

The original Ag/W-only files are in [`xtant3_ag_w_source/`](xtant3_ag_w_source/). The accompanying upstream README, `.gitattributes`, and GPL-3.0 license file are preserved beside them. The README describes the tables as XTANT-3 results and points to Medvedev and coauthors' papers; it also says the data are for non-commercial peaceful use. Keep that notice with any copy and check the source terms before use outside research/education.

| File | Quantity and columns | Grid in supplied file |
|---|---|---:|
| `Ce_Ag.txt` | Dynamical-simulation `Ce(Te)`, J m⁻³ K⁻¹ | 600–23,000 K, 100 K; 225 rows |
| `Ce_W.txt` | Dynamical-simulation `Ce(Te)`, J m⁻³ K⁻¹ | 500–19,000 K, 100 K; 186 rows |
| `G_e-ph_Ag.txt` | `G(Te)`, W m⁻³ K⁻¹ | 600–23,000 K, 100 K; 225 rows |
| `G_e-ph_W.txt` | `G(Te)`, W m⁻³ K⁻¹ | 500–19,000 K, 100 K; 186 rows |
| `K_Ag.dat`, `K_W.dat` | `Te`, total `ke`, electron-phonon and electron-electron conductivity contributions, chemical potential, and a `Ce` column | 300–50,000 K, 100 K; 498 rows each |

The supplied `Ce` and `G` files have two columns each. In particular, these Ag/W `G` files do not contain an atomic/lattice-temperature column. They therefore provide `G(Te)` only, not a measured or calculated `G(Te,Tl)` surface.

The `K` tables' `Ce` columns are **not the same dataset** as `Ce_Ag.txt` and `Ce_W.txt`: the upstream README says the `K`-table heat capacities were calculated for ideal crystals with a 7×7×7 k-point mesh, while the standalone `Ce` files are from dynamical simulations at the gamma point. Preserve these as alternatives instead of splicing them together. The `K` tables extend to 50,000 K, but that does not extend the supplied `G` or standalone `Ce` validity range.

## Numerical cross-checks

The reproducible inventory and exact per-file SHA-256 values are in [`XTANT3_AG_W_SOURCE_AUDIT.json`](XTANT3_AG_W_SOURCE_AUDIT.json), generated by [`audit_xtant_ag_w_tables.py`](../scripts/audit_xtant_ag_w_tables.py). The Lin W comparison uses the separately digitized candidate curves in [`W_TTM_Lin2008_digitized_300_20000K.csv`](W_TTM_Lin2008_digitized_300_20000K.csv); those points are graph digitization, not original author tabular data.

| Check | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| W standalone `Ce` at 500 K: XTANT / Lin digitized | 92.2 / 68.7 kJ m⁻³ K⁻¹ | XTANT is 1.34× the digitized Lin curve at this point. |
| W standalone `Ce` at 11,000 K: XTANT / Lin digitized | 2.829 / 2.843 MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹ | Close at this point; agreement does not erase the low-temperature source/method difference. |
| W standalone `Ce` at 600 K / W `K`-table `Ce` | 105.2 / 59.0 kJ m⁻³ K⁻¹ | The two XTANT heat-capacity products differ by 1.78× at 600 K. |
| Ag standalone `Ce` at 600 K / Ag `K`-table `Ce` | 18.7 / 38.9 kJ m⁻³ K⁻¹ | The two XTANT products differ by about a factor of 2 at the lower edge. |
| W `G` at 600 K: XTANT / Lin digitized | 1.71×10¹⁶ / 2.35×10¹⁷ W m⁻³ K⁻¹ | XTANT is 7.3% of the Lin digitization and 12.2% of Duffy's 1.4×10¹⁷ low-temperature experimental anchor. |
| W `G` at 11,000 K: XTANT / Lin digitized | 1.97×10¹⁷ / 1.01×10¹⁸ W m⁻³ K⁻¹ | XTANT is about 19.6% of the Lin digitization. |
| W `G` at 19,000 K: XTANT / Lin digitized | 2.29×10¹⁷ / 1.19×10¹⁸ W m⁻³ K⁻¹ | XTANT is about 19.2% of the Lin digitization. |

These are materially different W electron-lattice coupling models. Do not average them or treat the XTANT curve as a correction to Lin. Keep the model identity attached to each run and compare them as sensitivity cases.

The `K`-table total conductivity is internally consistent with the reciprocal combination of the listed electron-phonon and electron-electron contributions, `1/ke = 1/ke-ph + 1/ke-e`; do not add those two columns arithmetically. At 300 K the tables give `ke,total = 16.56 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹` for Ag and `30.09 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹` for W. These are candidate XTANT values, not independent validation against bulk conductivity. For W, Duffy et al.'s Drude closure has a separate 300 K electron-conductivity anchor of 130.5 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹; retain it as another model alternative rather than silently replacing the table.

## Candidate use and limits

- A common in-range window for the standalone Ag/W `Ce` and `G` files is 600–19,000 K. The `K` tables cover 300–50,000 K. Any use below/above the selected source's range needs an explicitly documented low-temperature model or boundary rule; do not infer coverage from another table's wider grid.
- For a simple exploratory TTM, possible per-element inputs are the standalone XTANT `Ce(Te)` and `G(Te)` files together with the XTANT total `ke(Te)` table. This mixes calculations with different stated methods for `Ce` and has no `Tl` dependence; mark it as provisional and compare against the alternate Lin/Duffy W cases.
- Another internally traceable choice for `Ce` is the `Ce` column bundled with `K`, but that choice is different from the standalone dynamical `Ce` dataset. A run must name which `Ce` was used.
- No Ag/W-specific electronic interfacial conductance is present. Li et al. (2024) reports Au/Pt and Au/Al values and demonstrates that interface condition/oxide can strongly change the result; it is a method analogue, not an Ag/W value.
- Pulse duration/envelope, absorbed fluence, and the mixed-phase/interface heat-transfer closure remain to be specified. Most critically, the full 3D QSGS candidate still fails the spring-free static-start audit. No 300 K MD, TTM, or melt/quench trajectory has been started.

## Related papers now archived

- Vijayakumar et al. (1988), “Calculated phase diagrams of Cu–W, Ag–W and Au–W binary systems,” *CALPHAD* 12(2), 177–184. The Ag–W diagram is a calculation: the paper states there was no experimentally determined phase diagram/thermodynamic data, assumes no mutual solid solubility, and models liquid equilibria with a subregular solution. Treat it as a model scenario, not a validated experimental phase diagram.
- Waseda and Ohtani (1974), “Static Structure of Liquid Noble and Transition Metals by X-Ray Diffraction,” *Physica Status Solidi (b)* 62, 535–546. Includes liquid Ag structure but not liquid W or an Ag–W liquid alloy.
- Torre et al. (2021), “Investigation on the Thermodynamic Stability of Nanocrystalline W-Based Alloys,” *Materials* 14, 7179, DOI 10.3390/ma14237179. Reports persistent W–Ag segregation and demixing after moderate-temperature annealing in the studied mechanically processed nanocrystalline material; it is useful qualitative evidence, not a laser-melt/quench phase diagram.
- Li et al. (2024), “Electronic interfacial thermal conductance between metal nano-films in sub-picosecond non-equilibrium transport process,” *International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer* 229, 125717, DOI 10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2024.125717. Reports `hee=4.4±1.8 GW m⁻² K⁻¹` for Au/Pt and `0.13±0.05 GW m⁻² K⁻¹` for Au/Al; not a direct Ag/W measurement.
