# Independent audit: Dai B2 benchmark and He interface references

## AgW B2: the Table 7 difference is reproducible but unresolved

Dai et al. Table 7 gives AgW B2 at a = 3.22 Å with cohesive energy 4.7990 eV/atom for “This work” and 4.7212 eV/atom for VASP. An independent lattice-shell sum at the printed lattice constant uses the B2 shells: eight Ag–W neighbors at sqrt(3)a/2, six same-species neighbors at a, and the twelve Ag third-shell neighbors at sqrt(2)a. The resulting EFS total energy is **−4.713085333161 eV/atom**, or cohesive energy 4.713085333161 eV/atom.

This shell sum does not use a neighbor-list implementation. It agrees with the independent radial-neighbor sum to 3.5e−14 eV/atom, and with LAMMPS `eam/fs` at the same lattice constant to 1.24e−12 eV/atom. Relaxing the cubic cell changes the lattice constant to 3.22004079 Å and does not materially change the energy. Thus the 0.0859147 eV/atom gap to the EFS number printed in Table 7 is not explained by the structure, pair counting, or EFS-to-LAMMPS conversion tested here. The computed value is 0.0081147 eV/atom from the VASP value.

The paper says the VASP alloy calculations used plane waves, Vanderbilt ultrasoft pseudopotentials, a Perdew–Wang GGA, and an 11×11×11 Monkhorst–Pack mesh. It does not state a plane-wave cutoff or fully specify the cohesive-energy reference and relaxation protocol for Table 7. The article also provides no input deck or EFS evaluation code. Those missing details prevent an exact reconstruction of the paper's calculation path. **No cross parameter was changed to force a match.** The shell audit is in `dai_2006_efs_validation_report.json`; its implementation is `scripts/validate_dai_efs_eamfs.py`.

## Interface references: current strain and cell conventions differ

He et al. (2018) define `W_sep` by separating an interface into free surfaces and define `gamma_int` using bulk references and subtraction of the two free-surface energies. Their Table 4 models are one-interface slabs embedded in vacuum. The paper reports seven atomic layers as sufficient for the surface-energy convergence tests, 15 Å vacuum between slabs, and about 3.44% in-plane mismatch for both Ag/W orientations. Its method is CASTEP/PBE, 360 eV cutoff, 9×9×9 bulk and 9×9×1 surface/interface k meshes, energy tolerance 5×10⁻⁵ eV/atom, force tolerance 0.03 eV/Å, and displacement tolerance 0.01 Å.

The EFS calculations here use periodic cells with two Ag/W boundaries, fixed in-plane dimensions, fixed bulk-like central cores, and gaps scanned at fixed cell height. The periodic-cell interface energy is `(E_cell − N_Ag e_Ag_bulk − N_W e_W_bulk)/(2A)`, an average over both boundaries. `W_sep` is also derived per boundary from the two-boundary cell. These definitions agree with a single-interface value only when the two boundaries are equivalent.

The current commensurate cells do not reproduce He et al.'s reported mismatch. Ag(100)/W(100) imposes +4.53% on Ag and −4.33% on W in each in-plane direction. Ag(111)/W(110) imposes Ag +2.02%/−0.83% and W −1.98%/+0.84%. The best sampled EFS values therefore remain screening results, not an apples-to-apples DFT reproduction:

| Interface | EFS W_sep / gamma_int (J/m²) | He et al. DFT (J/m²) | Absolute difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ag(100)/W(100) | 2.894 / 1.127 | 2.80 / 0.72 | +0.094 / +0.407 |
| Ag(111)/W(110) | 3.222 / 0.127 | 2.45 / 0.86 | +0.772 / −0.733 |

## Are the two periodic boundaries equivalent?

The selected minimized Ag(100)/W(100) cell has equal Ag/W atom counts in 5 Å windows around both boundaries, identical cross-pair distance histograms, and a local EAM per-atom excess-energy difference of zero at the reported precision. The two boundaries are equivalent by these checks.

For the selected Ag(111)/W(110) cell, the 5 Å windows have equal Ag/W atom counts. Their cross-pair counts differ by 2.69%, normalized distance histograms by 3.54%, and minimum Ag–W distances by 0.00178 Å. Their windowed EAM per-atom excess energies differ by 0.03395 J/m². These metrics show a small but measurable boundary asymmetry; the reported total-cell thermodynamics is the average of the two. The local-energy split is diagnostic rather than a unique thermodynamic partition. Full results and limitations are in `two_interface_equivalence_audit.json`, generated by `scripts/audit_periodic_interface_equivalence.py`.

## What must change for a direct DFT comparison

Use a one-interface slab with vacuum, or a demonstrably symmetric two-interface periodic cell; match the DFT in-plane cell and strain; reproduce the stated number of atomic layers and terminations; use the same bulk and surface reference conventions; and relax the interface and free surfaces consistently. Until then, treat the EFS-to-DFT differences as a combined potential-plus-cell/reference discrepancy. This audit does not validate the EFS potential for quantitative interface predictions.
